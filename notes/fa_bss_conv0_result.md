# FA-BSS-CONV0 result — 2026-08-24

## Gate

CONV0 was the controlled follow-up to REAL1B.

REAL1B's real AuxIVA target looked broadband in raw complex ratios but collapsed under a best-constant-direction attacker. CONV0 therefore froze a synthetic 2x2 FIR mixing world **before morphology training**:

```text
x1[t] = s1[t] + 0.90 * s2[t - 14]
x2[t] = 0.35 * s1[t - 3] + s2[t]
```

The exact source-1 extraction direction is

```text
w(omega) = [1, -0.90 exp(-i 14 omega)]
```

across nine fixed frequencies `omega = linspace(0.07, 0.35, 9)`.

The scientific target, thresholds, four seeds, proposal budgets, and control structure were committed before the first run. See [`conv0_rotating_demixer_contract.md`](conv0_rotating_demixer_contract.md).

The run executed in GitHub Actions on Python 3.12 against current `FunctionalArbors/main`.

Workflow run: `32705579032`.

## Algebra audit

The synthetic mixer behaved as designed:

```text
minimum determinant magnitude   0.710
maximum determinant magnitude   1.312
maximum interferer leakage      0.000e+00
```

So the mixing matrix was comfortably invertible across the whole grid and the analytic target row cancelled `s2` exactly to numerical precision.

## C0 — constant-direction hardness passes

The best single frequency-independent complex direction scored:

```text
31.376 degrees mean projective error
```

The pre-registered hardness prerequisite was `>= 30 degrees`, so the morphology experiment was allowed to proceed.

This is the first Monday broadband target that is known in advance not to collapse to the constant-vector loophole that killed REAL1B.

## C1 — matched FIR remains the boring winner

The exact digital extractor is

```text
y[t] = x1[t] - 0.90 * x2[t - 14]
```

Its measured target error was effectively zero:

```text
0.000115 degrees
```

The tiny nonzero value is numerical projective-angle precision, not a meaningful residual.

Nothing in CONV0 beats ordinary signal processing. This target is deliberately generated by one gain and one delay, so a matched FIR is the correct simple solution.

## Shared-body arm

One fixed-mass FunctionalArbor morphology had to improve all nine rotating target directions simultaneously.

```text
seed 0   50.65 -> 35.14 deg
seed 1   39.65 -> 35.85 deg
seed 2   50.94 -> 31.18 deg
seed 3   60.86 -> 31.14 deg
```

Aggregate:

```text
start mean                       50.52 deg
shared final mean                33.33 deg
median fractional improvement     34.7%
seeds improved                    4 / 4
seeds beating constant attacker   2 / 4
```

This is materially stronger than REAL1B in one sense: the target is genuinely rotating and every shared body moved substantially toward it.

But the pre-registered main representation gate required at least 3/4 seeds to beat the 31.376-degree constant-direction attacker. Only seeds 2 and 3 did so.

Therefore:

```text
rotating_representation_pass = false
```

This is a near miss, not a pass.

The correct statement is:

> One shared morphology can learn a substantial part of a known rotating demixing curve, but the current v0.5 morphology/search did not reliably outperform the best constant complex direction across seeds.

## C2 — independent-body oracle

Allowing each frequency its own separately remodeled body gave:

```text
mean independent-body error = 23.21 deg
```

That is clearly better than both the shared-body mean (`33.33 deg`) and the constant-direction attacker (`31.38 deg`).

This is useful diagnostically.

The morphology family/search can reach many of the individual required directions. The remaining difficulty is not simply that FunctionalArbors cannot represent the target vectors at all. A substantial part of the problem is satisfying them **with one shared geometry**.

It is still nowhere near the exact matched FIR.

## C3 — held-out frequencies passes

The shared body was trained only on indices `0,2,4,6,8`. The interleaved frequencies `1,3,5,7` never entered the mutation-acceptance objective.

Held-out results:

```text
seed 0   48.92 -> 45.09 deg
seed 1   41.30 -> 30.22 deg
seed 2   56.55 -> 38.33 deg
seed 3   52.75 -> 39.47 deg
```

Aggregate:

```text
held-out start mean              49.88 deg
held-out final mean              38.28 deg
seeds improved                    4 / 4
median held-out improvement      12.18 deg
```

All four seeds improved frequencies that were never used to decide whether a structural mutation survived.

That satisfies the pre-registered first two parts of the coupling gate.

## C4 — reversed-frequency control

Before the run, the wrong target control was frozen to the exact same nine target vectors in reversed frequency order.

When that wrong curve was optimized and the result was scored against the correct curve:

```text
correct-curve mean   50.52 -> 43.91 deg
median improvement             4.90 deg
```

The held-out arm's median unseen-frequency improvement (`12.18 deg`) was substantially larger than the correct-curve improvement produced by optimizing the reversed target (`4.90 deg`).

Therefore:

```text
heldout_coupling_pass = true
```

This is the strongest surviving result of CONV0.

REAL1B had only a `coupling_hint` on a weak real target. CONV0 shows the same phenomenon on a target whose frequency rotation was fixed analytically before training:

> **Structural mutations selected using some frequencies can move unseen frequencies in the useful direction, and they do so more strongly when the frequency ordering is correct than when the same targets are reversed.**

That is operational evidence that the coefficients generated by one body are genuinely coupled through shared structure.

## What did not survive

Do not say:

> One FunctionalArbor reliably beats a constant demixer on a rotating broadband target.

It did not. Only 2/4 seeds beat the constant attacker, so the pre-registered representation gate failed.

Do not say:

> Structural morphology is a more efficient representation than an FIR filter.

It plainly is not on this target. The matched FIR is exact and simple.

Do not say:

> CONV0 demonstrates blind structural source separation.

The target row is supplied directly to the morphology acceptance objective.

## What survives now

Three progressively stronger observations are now on record:

1. **REAL0:** morphology can embody one real complex demixing coefficient.
2. **REAL1B:** one body can move several frequency coefficients together; held-out improvement appeared on a weak real target.
3. **CONV0:** on a predeclared genuinely rotating target, held-out frequencies improved in 4/4 seeds and correct frequency ordering mattered more than a reversed ordering.

The shared-body representation itself is still not good enough to pass the main attacker.

So Monday's current strongest statement is no longer merely verbal:

> **One persistent geometry imposes a learnable coupling across a family of frequency responses.**

The unresolved question is whether that coupling can ever become a *useful parameterization* rather than merely a constraint.

## What should be attacked next

CONV0's exact target is intentionally low-dimensional:

```text
ratio(omega) = -0.90 exp(-i 14 omega)
```

One gain + one delay generates the whole curve. That is why the matched FIR is trivial.

The next discriminating synthetic gate should therefore increase the target's internal structure **without changing it after seeing the arbor**. A clean candidate is a multi-tap cross-path, for example a fixed sum of two or three delayed echoes, with coefficients and delays pre-registered only after checking:

1. the full 2x2 mixer is safely invertible;
2. the best constant direction is poor;
3. a one-delay phase-ramp model is also poor;
4. the exact ordinary FIR inverse remains available as the calibration winner.

Then ask again whether one body beats the reduced digital models and whether held-out frequencies generalize.

But that is a new gate. CONV0 itself is frozen here.

## Bottom line

CONV0 did not give the clean representation win.

It gave something narrower and more robust:

**the cross-frequency coupling is real enough to generalize to frequencies not used by the structural selection rule.**

That is now the surviving object to understand.
